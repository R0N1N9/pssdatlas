Drug-safety signals in FDA adverse-event reports

Reporting odds ratio (ROR) with 95% confidence interval: how much more often a sexual adverse event is reported with each drug than with all other drugs in the database. Values above 1 (the vertical line) mean disproportionate reporting. Comparator drugs with no expected effect are shown in grey.

Spontaneous reports cannot establish causation or incidence. They are affected by publicity, by the underlying illness (depression itself impairs sexual function) and by under-reporting. The database does not record whether a symptom persisted after the drug was stopped, so these figures describe sexual adverse events in general, not PSSD specifically.

Drug targets

Two starting points for drug discovery: which proteins the implicated drugs actually bind, and how workable each candidate target is.

What the implicated drugs bind

Binding strength (pKi) from published laboratory measurements: 9 is very strong, 7 is moderate, below 6 is weak. Darker means stronger. A dot means no measurement was found, which is not the same as no binding.

Binding data are incomplete and uneven: well-studied drugs such as clozapine have far more measurements than newer ones, and many cells rest on a single experiment. Binding says nothing about whether a drug activates or blocks a protein, or about lasting effects after it is stopped.

Candidate target dossiers

For each candidate: marketed drugs that already act on it, and its expression in available tissues (transcripts per million). No sensory-ganglion tissue is available in this dataset, so sensory-nerve channels appear low here regardless of their real importance.

Patient reports and community sources

What people describe in online communities: the drugs they took, their symptoms, what they tried and how they say it went. Collected from public posts; usernames and post text are not stored.
